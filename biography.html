Beginning

Acting found her through a moment of destiny.

Mina Lakani entered the world of theatre on her eighteenth birthday, at the beginning of her nineteenth year, when an unexpected invitation led her to rehearsals for Shakespeare’s Hamlet. That first encounter marked the beginning of a professional acting career that would span nearly two decades across theatre, cinema, and television in Iran.

Professional Years

A professional life across stage and screen.

Over the years that followed, Lakani worked across theatre, cinema, television, televised theatre, and screen projects. Her career developed through a combination of stage discipline, lead and supporting screen work, and a continuing interest in performance as a serious artistic practice.

The surviving photographs, credits, published interviews, and critical writing preserved on this website document only part of that professional history, but together they form a record of a sustained acting career.

Challenges & Restrictions

The work existed within limits that shaped the life around it.

Lakani’s professional years in Iran unfolded within a cultural system in which artists, and women in particular, worked under a range of legal, institutional, and social restrictions. Some of those realities remain visible in the archival record itself, including the compulsory dress requirements imposed on women in public and professional media.

As her career progressed, the distance between professional opportunities and her personal values and convictions became increasingly apparent. Over time, that conflict deepened until it could no longer be ignored.

Turning Point

A career can continue while still reaching a point of departure.

For Lakani, the decisive question eventually became larger than the next role. Continuing within the professional world she had built increasingly conflicted with what she was willing to accept personally and ethically. She ultimately chose to leave Iran in 2010, stepping away from a professional life she had spent nearly twenty years establishing.

The years that followed were not simply a continuation of the same career in another country. They became a different chapter, shaped by displacement, rebuilding, and the effort to remain faithful to her convictions.

Public Voice

Leaving Iran: a public testimony.

Broadcast appearances and interviews form another part of Lakani’s documented public history. Surviving material includes a formal Sahar International television interview and her September 2011 appearance on Voice of America Persian’s Shabahang, hosted by Behnood Mokri, a prominent VOA Persian presenter. At the time, Shabahang was described as one of VOA Persian’s most-watched programs, with a substantial following in Iran and among Persian-speaking audiences abroad.

In that interview, Lakani spoke publicly about the circumstances that led her to leave Iran, giving a first-person account of the decision that changed the course of her life and career.

The surviving recordings are preserved here as documentary material: the complete Shabahang interview, together with a focused excerpt in which Mokri asks Lakani about the emotional difficulty of leaving Iran after building her professional life across theatre, television, and cinema.

Perspective

What remains after the roles.

Looking back, Mina Lakani does not measure an artistic life only by roles, recognition, or public visibility. For her, a career also carries a responsibility to remain answerable to conscience. When continuing within Iran’s state-controlled cultural system came into conflict with that principle, she chose to leave the professional world she had built rather than remain within a system she could no longer accept.

That choice did not make the years that followed easier, nor has she ever sought to portray it as an act of heroism. The decision came at a profound professional cost. Yet, despite the consequences, she has remained faithful to the convictions that guided her choice, and her conscience remains clear.

Her experience has left her with a belief that transcends the boundaries of any country or profession:

Success loses its true meaning when it comes at the cost of personal integrity and moral conscience.

This archive preserves not only the work, but also the choices that shaped the life around it.
